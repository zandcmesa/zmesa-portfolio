Payments onboarding in emerging markets.

Year

2024

My Role

UX Manager

Payments UX/UI

The Challenge

Pixel is Google's flagship phone. Setup is the first thing you see when you turn a new one on, and it's where people get into the Google ecosystem and make the phone their own. It matters a lot.


Our job was to let people add local forms of payment to Google Pay during setup in two emerging markets. We needed to support UPI in India and direct carrier billing (DCB) in Indonesia. That meant really understanding how those payment methods work, and making sure it all worked on every new Pixel on launch day.

Project Outcome

Significant lift in saved payment methods

After launch, the number of payment methods saved to Google went up significantly, which was one of our main goals. More saved payment methods means people can pay more easily anywhere Google Pay works.

Material, and our own constraints

We had to balance the latest Material Design standards with what our Payments Platform could actually support. Dynamic color meant adopting Material's newest style tokens, so the flow could pick up the color profile of whichever device you were setting up.

Usable with a screen reader, not just compliant

Passing an accessibility audit isn't the same as being usable with a screen reader. We worked closely with engineering and delivered "greenlines" (annotated accessibility specs) alongside our designs.

All devices, all orientations

One of the hardest parts was designing for devices whose specs were still being finalized while we worked. We stayed in close contact with the Pixel team so we always had the latest requirements for every form factor.
